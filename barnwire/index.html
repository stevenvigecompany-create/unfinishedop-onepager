BarnWire — Issue 1

Week of Oct 5, 2026 · 21 sale barns · TX, OK, AR, LA, KS

Howdy. This is BarnWire, a short weekly letter on where calves brought the most money.

One number per barn: the price for a 550-lb steer (500–600 lb class), as each barn reported it for the week of Oct 5. The average across 20 barns with that class was $3.59/lb ($359/cwt). We show how many dollars a head each barn paid above or below that average on a 550-lb steer.

Paid the MOST this week (550-lb steer)

  1. Tulia, TX: $4.33/lb, +$403 a head over average (USDA)
  2. Wildorado (Lonestar), TX: $4.21/lb, +$340 a head (USDA)
  3. Apache (Stockman), OK: $4.02/lb, +$234 a head (USDA AMS 1829 — switched from FB image this week)
  4. Dalhart (Cattlemen's), TX: $3.96/lb, +$203 a head (USDA)
  5. San Saba (Jordan), TX: $3.79/lb, +$108 a head (Choice-grade quotes)

Paid the LEAST

  1. Canton (FCCF), TX: $2.85/lb, −$409 a head (handwritten sheet, low confidence)
  2. Coushatta (Red River), LA: $2.93/lb, −$365 a head
  3. Ada (SOLA), OK: $3.10/lb, −$272 a head (9/30 report)
  4. Emory, TX: $3.25/lb, −$189 a head
  5. Mount Pleasant (Stone), TX: $3.40/lb, −$107 a head

Biggest like-for-like gaps (same class + weight band)

  • 600–700 lb steers: Tulia $3.92/lb vs Coushatta (Red River, LA) $2.63/lb. That's $1.29/lb, about $838 a head on a 650-lb steer.
  • 500–600 lb steers: Tulia $4.33/lb vs Canton (FCCF) $2.85/lb. That's $1.48/lb, about $812 a head on a 550-lb steer (FCCF sheet is low-confidence handwriting). vs Coushatta at $2.93/lb the gap is still about $770/hd.
  • 400–500 lb heifers: Carthage $4.35/lb vs Canton (FCCF) $2.98/lb. Emory, about 90 miles west of Carthage, paid $3.25/lb on the same class.
  • East Texas neighbors: Carthage and Emory sell the same day. Carthage beat Emory by about 45¢/lb on 500–600 lb steers ($3.70 vs $3.25), about $248 a head on a 550-lb steer.

State notes

  • Texas: The Panhandle (Tulia, Lonestar, Dalhart) keeps paying up. Carthage stood out in East Texas. Jordan (Hill Country) was strong on Choice calves. Columbus, Calvert, Mount Pleasant (Stone) and Canton (FCCF) lagged. FCCF's handwritten sheet is hard to read — treat as low-confidence.
  • Oklahoma: Apache (Stockman, now USDA) and Waurika both solid and above the field. Ada (SOLA) came in low, but its report is from 9/30 and hard to read.
  • Arkansas: Hope was $3.40/lb on 550-lb steers (−$107/hd), near the middle on 5- and 6-weight heifers.
  • Louisiana: Lafayette (Dominique's) paid $3.60/lb (+$3/hd), Amite $3.49/lb (−$57/hd, 9/22 report) and Coushatta $2.93/lb (−$365/hd). Wide spreads at Coushatta pulled averages down. Kinder posted no prices. Dominique's website still shows Sep 28–29 prices (unchanged this week).
  • Kansas: La Crosse posted cows at $1.09–1.45/lb and bulls at $1.63–1.71/lb only, with no feeder prices yet.

How we figure it

For each barn we take the middle of its posted low-to-high price on 500–600 lb steers (or the USDA head-weighted average where available), compare it to the all-barn average, and multiply the difference by 550 lbs. A full 0–100 score that covers every class is in barn_scores_2026-10.csv.

Read with care: most barns post a low-to-high range. USDA barns and Columbus report averages instead, and a range midpoint is not the same thing as an average. Quality, flesh and lot size vary barn to barn. Full rows and flags are in barn_reports_2026-10.csv.
