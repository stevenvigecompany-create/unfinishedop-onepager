01

Buyback Principle

Don't hire to grow the business. Hire to buy back your time—so growth frees the calendar instead of filling it.

How

When capacity hurts, ask "whose hours am I buying?" not "who can handle more volume." Trade money for low-value hours so you keep the high-value ones.

Example (UOP-original)

A solo newsletter founder hires a VA for inbox and scheduling at $15/hr instead of hiring a second writer first—reclaiming mornings for issue drafting.

Source: Buy Back Your Time (Dan Martell, 2023), Ch. 1 "How I Buy Back My Life" (Buyback Principle). Paraphrased.

02

Buyback Rate

Set a dollar-per-hour floor for what you should still do yourself: (annual personal income ÷ 2,000 hours) ÷ 4.

How

Add salary + profit take-home. Divide by 2,000 for an effective hourly rate, then divide by 4 for a 4× return target. Outsource anything you can buy below that rate.

Example (UOP-original)

$120,000 take-home → $60/hr effective → $15 Buyback Rate. Booking travel and filing receipts both leave the founder's calendar.

Source: Buy Back Your Time, Ch. 1–2 (Buyback Rate formula). Also described on buybackyourtime.com. Paraphrased.

03

DRIP Matrix

Sort every task by money and energy into four quadrants: Delegation, Replacement, Investment, Production—then live mostly in Production.

How

Plot tasks: low money + low joy → Delegation (jettison first); high money + low joy → Replacement; low money + high joy → Investment; high money + high joy → Production (keep).

Example (UOP-original)

Invoice chasing lands in Delegation; sales calls that pay but drain go in Replacement; weekend runs go in Investment; offer design stays in Production.

Source: Buy Back Your Time, Ch. 2 "The DRIP Matrix". Paraphrased.

04

Buyback Loop (Audit → Transfer → Fill)

Repeat a three-step loop forever: audit time and energy, transfer low-value drains, then fill the freed hours with high-value work—not more busywork.

How

Log ~15-minute blocks for two weeks. Mark energy green/red and value with $–$$$$. Transfer red, cheap tasks first. Protect the new open blocks for Production or Investment only.

Example (UOP-original)

After a two-week log, a shop owner moves inventory counts and supplier emails off the calendar, then fills Tuesday mornings with product R&D.

Source: Buy Back Your Time, Ch. 1 (Buyback Loop: audit, transfer, fill). Paraphrased.

05

Replacement Ladder

Hire in a fixed order so you don't burn out managing the wrong roles: Admin → Delivery → Marketing → Sales → Leadership.

How

At each rung, name the key hire, the ownership they take (inbox/calendar; onboarding/support; campaigns/traffic; calls/follow-up; strategy/outcomes), and only then climb. Role beats title.

Example (UOP-original)

An agency skips Admin and hires a closer first—then spends nights fixing CRM data. Climbing Admin → Delivery first would have prevented that.

Source: Buy Back Your Time, Ch. 5 "The Replacement Ladder". Paraphrased.

06

Camcorder Method & 4Cs Playbooks

Train by recording yourself doing the work, then turn it into a playbook: Camcorder, Course, Cadence, Checklist—often drafted by the person you hired.

How

Screen-record (or phone-record) the task 3+ times while narrating why. Have the hire write the step list, set how often it runs, and add a must-hit checklist. Start with one high-ROI playbook.

Example (UOP-original)

A founder Looms three client-onboarding runs; the new CS lead writes the course and checklist and owns onboarding thereafter.

Source: Buy Back Your Time, Ch. 7 "Building Playbooks" (Camcorder Method; Four Cs). Paraphrased.

07

Perfect Week

Design the ideal recurring week on purpose—theme days, stack similar work, match tasks to energy peaks—then treat that calendar as the default.

How

Block deep work where energy is highest. Batch meetings and admin. Cut buffer gaps that invite drift, end on time to stop bleed into the next block, and reduce context switching.

Example (UOP-original)

Mon/Wed = writing only; Tue = sales calls; Thu = team syncs; Fri = review and planning. Unscheduled Slack time disappears.

Source: Buy Back Your Time, Ch. 8 "Your Perfect Week". Paraphrased.

08

Four Time Hacks

Four rules that stop you from becoming the bottleneck: 1:3:1, Definition of Done, a small spend allowance ("Magic Pill"), and sync meetings with a repeat agenda.

How

Before anyone asks for help: one problem, three options, one recommendation. State done as facts, feelings, and functionality. Give a fixed spend limit so small fixes don't need you. Run AA syncs on a standing agenda.

Example (UOP-original)

Ops brings one shipping delay, three carriers, and a pick—plus a $50 allowance to overnight the package without a Slack ping.

Source: Buy Back Your Time, Ch. 9 "The Only 4 Time Hacks You Need". Also listed on buybackyourtime.com. Paraphrased.

09

Test-First Hiring

Don't hire on interviews alone—pay finalists to do a real, representative task before you offer the role.

How

Cast wide, require a short intro video, use a profile assessment, then pay 2–3 finalists for a test project with light instructions. Sell the future to the winner once you've seen the work.

Example (UOP-original)

Three VA finalists each get paid to process one week's receipts and draft a summary; the clearest, fastest draft wins the seat.

Source: Buy Back Your Time, Ch. 10 "The 'Test-First' Hiring Method". Paraphrased.

10

10-80-10 Rule

Stay in the work only at the edges: you set the first 10% (vision and constraints), the team does the middle 80%, you return for the last 10% polish.

How

Open with outcome, Definition of Done, and limits. Hand off. Review the nearly finished draft and apply the final quality pass—don't rebuild from blank every time.

Example (UOP-original)

Founder sketches landing-page goals and must-haves; a contractor builds the page to 80%; founder tunes headline and proof, then ships.

Source: Buy Back Your Time (10-80-10 Rule; commonly paired with cloning/playbook chapters, Ch. 6–7). Paraphrased; exact chapter title varies across public summaries.
