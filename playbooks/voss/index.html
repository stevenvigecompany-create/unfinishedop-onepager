01

Mirroring

Repeat the last one to three critical words your counterpart just said, then pause—so they keep talking and fill in the blanks.

How

Listen for the key phrase. Mirror it softly (often with a calm, late-night DJ tone). Stay quiet for a few seconds. Let them expand, correct, or reveal what they meant.

Example (UOP-original)

Vendor: "We can't move the delivery before mid-month." You: "Mid-month?" Silence. They add the real constraint: a warehouse crew is booked solid until the 18th.

Source: Never Split the Difference (Chris Voss with Tahl Raz, 2016), Ch. 2 "Be a Mirror". Paraphrased.

02

Labeling (Tactical Empathy)

Name the emotion you observe—without agreeing with it—so negatives lose heat and the other side feels understood.

How

Spot the feeling under the words. Say it as a neutral label: "It seems like…", "It sounds like…", or "It looks like…". Avoid "I think…" (that centers you). Then go silent and let them respond.

Example (UOP-original)

A client snaps about a delayed invoice. You: "It seems like you're worried this will mess up your month-end numbers." They exhale and explain the real deadline.

Source: Never Split the Difference, Ch. 3 "Don't Feel Their Pain, Label It". Paraphrased.

03

Accusation Audit

List the worst things they could say about you—or your motives—and say them first, so the sting is gone before they attack.

How

Before the hard conversation, write every harsh judgment they might hold. Open with the top few as labels ("You probably think we're lowballing you…"). Let them correct you. Then move into the real ask.

Example (UOP-original)

Before asking a landlord to break a lease early: "You probably think we're flaky tenants trying to dump costs on you." The landlord softens and asks what timeline you need.

Source: Never Split the Difference, Ch. 3 "Don't Feel Their Pain, Label It" (accusation audit). Paraphrased.

04

Master "No"

Treat "No" as the start of the negotiation, not the end—because it makes people feel safe and in control.

How

Don't push for early "Yes" (it often means "stop bothering me"). Invite a clear "No" when stuck ("Have you given up on this project?"). After "No," ask what doesn't work or what they don't want, then rebuild from there.

Example (UOP-original)

A prospect ghosts after a demo. Email: "Have you given up on launching this quarter?" They reply "No—just buried," and rebook.

Source: Never Split the Difference, Ch. 4 "Beware 'Yes'—Master 'No'". Paraphrased.

05

"That's Right"

Earn the two words "That's right"—not "You're right"—by summarizing their world so accurately they feel truly understood.

How

Combine paraphrase (facts as they see them) with labels (emotions underneath). Deliver the summary, then pause. "That's right" signals ownership and breakthrough; "You're right" is often a polite brush-off.

Example (UOP-original)

After a tense scope fight: "So you're saying the launch date is fixed, the budget is locked, and you're worried extras will blow both—and that feels unfair after the last change order." Client: "That's right." Now you can redesign the package.

Source: Never Split the Difference, Ch. 5 "Trigger the Two Words That Immediately Transform Any Negotiation". Paraphrased.

06

Bend Their Reality

Shape what feels fair and urgent—deadlines, loss aversion, and "fair" framing—so the other side's gut moves toward your deal without a fight.

How

Don't hide your deadline (hidden deadlines often make you concede against yourself). Use an accusation audit to anchor fear of loss. Treat "fair" carefully: mirror or label weaponized fairness; set your own fair standard early. Prefer ranges and non-round numbers when you must name price.

Example (UOP-original)

Freelance rate talk: open with "I want this to feel fair on both sides—tell me if it doesn't," then propose $4,750–$5,800 with a clear delivery date instead of a round $5,000 ask.

Source: Never Split the Difference, Ch. 6 "Bend Their Reality". Paraphrased.

07

Calibrated Questions

Ask open "How?" and "What?" questions so the other side designs the solution—and feels in control—while you guide the path.

How

Avoid closed yes/no traps and accusatory "Why?" Prefer: "How am I supposed to do that?", "What's the biggest challenge here?", "How would you like me to proceed?" Ask genuinely, then stay quiet while they solve your problem.

Example (UOP-original)

Buyer demands a 40% cut. You: "How am I supposed to deliver that quality at that price?" They start listing which features they can drop instead of walking.

Source: Never Split the Difference, Ch. 7 "Create the Illusion of Control". Paraphrased.

08

Guarantee Execution

A "Yes" means nothing without a workable "How"—so stress-test implementation, spot deal-killers off the table, and confirm commitment three ways.

How

Ask calibrated implementation questions: "How will we know we're on track?" "How does this affect the rest of your team?" Watch for "I'll try" or "You're right." Use the Rule of Three: get the same agreement three times in different forms (summary → "That's right," then a how-question, then a concrete next step).

Example (UOP-original)

After verbal yes on a vendor switch: "How on board is your ops lead who's not on this call?" Silence reveals the real blocker—schedule a three-way before signing.

Source: Never Split the Difference, Ch. 8 "Guarantee Execution". Paraphrased.

09

Ackerman Bargaining

When it's time to haggle numbers, use a planned ladder—65% → 85% → 95% → 100% of your target—with empathy and calibrated "No"s between steps.

How

Set your real target first. Open at ~65%. After each counter, use labels and "How am I supposed to…?" so they bid against themselves before you raise. Land at 100% with a precise non-round number plus a small non-monetary sweeter they likely don't want—signaling you're at the limit.

Example (UOP-original)

Target buy price $10,000 for used equipment. Open $6,500; after calibrated pushback offer $8,500, then $9,500, then $9,850 plus throwing in pickup yourself.

Source: Never Split the Difference, Ch. 9 "Bargain Hard" (Ackerman model). Paraphrased.

10

Find the Black Swan

Hunt the unknown unknowns—hidden facts that, once revealed, change the whole negotiation—by listening past what you already expect.

How

Hold multiple hypotheses. Prefer face time over email. When something seems "crazy," dig (different info, hidden constraints, or other interests). Watch unguarded moments. Use open questions and labels to surface leverage you didn't know existed (positive, negative, or normative).

Example (UOP-original)

A "price-only" seller keeps stalling. After "It seems like something off the sheet is blocking this," they admit a non-compete with a sibling company—once named, a carve-out closes the deal.

Source: Never Split the Difference, Ch. 10 "Find the Black Swan". Paraphrased.
